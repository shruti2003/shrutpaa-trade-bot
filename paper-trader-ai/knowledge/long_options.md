# Buying Calls and Puts

## Long calls
Buying a call gives you the right to buy 100 shares at the strike before expiration. Your max loss is the premium paid. Breakeven at expiration = strike + premium. It is a leveraged bullish bet: you need the stock to move up enough, fast enough, to beat time decay.

## Long puts
Buying a put gives you the right to sell 100 shares at the strike. Use it to bet on a decline, or to hedge shares you own (a protective put). Breakeven = strike - premium. Max loss is the premium.

## Common mistakes
- Buying far out-of-the-money, short-dated options because they are cheap. Most expire worthless.
- Ignoring IV. Buying right before earnings often loses money to the IV crush, even if the direction is right.
- Over-sizing. Because options are leveraged, risking more than 1-2% of the account on a single long option trade can lead to fast drawdowns.

## Choosing contracts
- A higher delta (0.60-0.80, in the money) behaves more like stock, with less decay as a percentage of the price.
- Give the trade time. Buying 60+ DTE and closing with 21+ DTE left avoids the steepest decay.
- Check liquidity: tight bid/ask spreads and meaningful open interest. Wide spreads are a hidden cost.
