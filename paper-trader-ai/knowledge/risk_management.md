# Risk Management and Trading Discipline

## Position sizing
- Risk a small, fixed fraction of the account per trade. A common choice is 1-2% max loss for speculative trades.
- For stock and short-put positions, cap any single name at about 5-10% of equity, measured on the assigned/notional value.
- Count options at notional exposure (delta x 100 x contracts x price), not just at the premium.

## Diversification
Avoid stacking correlated positions, for example several large-cap tech names, all on the same side. A market-wide selloff will hit them all at once.

## Plan every trade
Before entering, write down the thesis, the entry, the profit target, the exit or stop condition, and what would prove you wrong. Then journal the outcome and the lesson. Reviewing your journal regularly is how paper trading turns into real skill.

## Treat paper money like real money
Paper trading is only useful if you size and behave as you would with real capital. Avoid "it's fake money" bets you would never make for real. Fills here assume you buy at the ask and sell at the bid, plus commissions, which is closer to real execution than mid-price fills.

## Common behavioral traps
- Revenge trading after a loss.
- Moving stops, or rolling losing short options indefinitely to avoid realizing a loss.
- Chasing premium on high-IV meme stocks with covered calls or CSPs.
- Concentrating in what has worked recently.
